Reverb and delay in the mix: creating space without washing it out

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

A dry mix sounds small, a wet one sounds distant. The difference is not how much reverb you use, but how you filter it, when it starts and how long it lasts.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  1. Sends, not inserts
  2. Filter the returns
  3. Pre-delay and timing
  4. Sidechain the effects
  5. Depth: who sits in front
  6. Electronic and club music
In short
  • Reverb and delay on sends, on dedicated buses, not as inserts on every track.
  • Filter the returns: high-pass between 200 and 400 Hz, low-pass between 6 and 10 kHz.
  • Pre-delay, decay and delay repeats timed to the BPM keep the space inside the groove.

Reverb is the fastest way to make a mix sound professional and the fastest way to ruin it. Too much reverb pushes the vocal back, swells the low end and blurs the mix. With a few simple rules, space becomes depth instead of fog.

Sends, not inserts

I use two or three shared effect buses: a short reverb (a room or a plate between 0.6 and 1.2 seconds), a long reverb (a hall between 1.8 and 3 seconds) and a delay. Each track sends the amount it needs. Everything lives in the same space and the mix sounds like one scene, not ten different rooms.

Filter the returns

Unfiltered reverb is the most common cause of a muddy mix. On the return I put a high-pass between 200 and 400 Hz and a low-pass between 6 and 10 kHz. Reverberated lows are almost never useful, and reverberated highs make every “s” in the vocal hiss.

Pre-delay and timing

Pre-delay is the gap before the tail starts. With 20 to 40 ms the vocal arrives dry and clear, then opens into the space: that is the trick that keeps a vocal in front even with a long reverb. Decay times and delay repeats also work better locked to the BPM: a dotted eighth or a quarter note sits in the groove, a random value smears it. The delay and reverb calculator gives you the values for your tempo.

The right reverb does not sound like an effect. You notice it when you remove it, because the mix gets small.

Sidechain the effects

A compressor on the reverb or delay return, keyed from the dry vocal, ducks the effects while the vocal sings and lets them bloom in the gaps. The vocal stays clear and the space shows up between phrases. 2 to 4 dB of reduction is enough.

Depth: who sits in front

More reverb and fewer highs push a track back; less reverb and more attack bring it forward. Decide first what sits in front, usually vocal, kick and bass, and let everything else fill the depth. I go deeper into this in the article on depth in the mix.

Electronic and club music

In club music the low end must stay mono and dry: no reverb below 150 to 200 Hz. On leads and pads, long reverbs with a sidechain from the kick work well, so the tail breathes with the groove instead of covering it.
