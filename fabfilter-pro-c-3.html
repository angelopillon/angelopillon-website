FabFilter Pro-C 3: 14 compression styles, Character and Auto Threshold

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

The compressor many engineers reach for first, me included, is now on version three. It was one of 2026’s most important releases for anyone who mixes.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  1. What’s new
  2. How I use it
  3. Sources
In short
  • Pro-C 3 was released on 15 January 2026.
  • Six new styles for a total of 14, plus three Character modes: Tube, Diode and Bright.
  • Auto Threshold, up to 32x oversampling, Atmos support up to 9.1.6. Price $199.

For years Pro-C 2 was the default compressor in countless sessions: transparent when needed, with different styles for vocals, buses and mastering. Pro-C 3 keeps the interface and widens the range.

What’s new

  • 14 compression styles, six more than Pro-C 2.
  • Character: three saturation modes, Tube, Diode and Bright, to add colour inside the compressor.
  • Auto Threshold: the threshold follows the signal level, so the amount of compression stays steady while you change the input gain.
  • Up to 32x oversampling and Atmos support up to 9.1.6.
  • Around the same time, Pro-Q 4.10 added the Instance List, to see and edit every open instance in one window.

How I use it

On vocals, a fast style with Auto Threshold on lets me change the gain without rebalancing the compression. On the mix bus I prefer a slow style with a few dB of reduction: details in the article on mix bus compression. The Character modes are handy, but careful: they add harmonics, and in a mix where you already saturate elsewhere they stack up.

A new compressor will not save a badly compressed vocal. A good one makes a well compressed vocal look easy.
