iZotope Nectar 5: Moments, Serial Compressor and Vocal Stabilizer, an engineer’s take

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

Boris FX has released the first Nectar since it acquired iZotope. Three of the new features genuinely change how a vocal gets mixed. One of them comes with a Logic limitation worth knowing before you buy.

By Angelo Pillon 26 November 2026 7 min read

In short
  • Nectar 5 has been available since 24 November 2026 in two editions: Nectar 5 at $249 and Nectar 5 Elements at $55, on sale for $179 and $39 until 7 December.
  • The features that matter: Moments, which applies effects to individual words from a transcription of the lyrics; Serial Compressor, up to three stages of compression, limiting and clipping in series; Vocal Stabilizer, an EQ that adapts as the vocal changes.
  • Moments only works in DAWs with ARA, so not in Ableton Live. In Logic Pro it runs only on Intel Macs or with Logic opened in Rosetta.

What Nectar 5 is

Nectar is iZotope’s vocal suite, from the same family as Ozone, RX and Neutron, which has belonged to Boris FX since July 2026. Version 5 brings the full package to 18 plugins: the main chain, called Mothership, 12 full modules, 2 mini-modules, Moments, Breath Control and Melodyne 5 essential.

All 14 modules also load as individual plugins. That matters more than it sounds: in a professional session you rarely use a closed chain. You take the piece you need, say only the de-esser or only the new compressor, and put it next to the tools you already use, analog ones included.

The three features that change the work

Moments: effects written on the lyrics

Moments is an ARA plugin that transcribes the vocal and shows the lyrics on the DAW timeline. You select words or phrases as you would in a text editor and apply effects: reverb and delay throws, reverse reverb swells, doubles, harmonies of up to four voices, pitch and formant shifts, distortion.

Anyone who mixes vocals knows how much time the same job takes today: dedicated sends, hand-drawn automation on the last word of the chorus, bounced phrases for doubling. Moments moves that work from the time grid to the text. For pop, R&B, rap and house or EDM top lines, where throws on key words are part of the arrangement, it is the most interesting feature of the release.

The best-supported languages include English, Spanish, Italian, German, French and Portuguese. Korean, Mandarin Chinese and Ukrainian are transcribed too, with more words to correct by hand.

Serial Compressor: series compression in a single plugin

Serial Compressor chains up to three compressors, limiters or clippers. There are four compressor styles, a limiter and a soft clipper, an oscilloscope to see what happens to the waveform, and a Learn button that sets makeup gain for you.

It is the technique studios have used with hardware for decades: a fast first stage that only catches the peaks, a slower second one that brings the vocal to a steady level. Each works lightly, so neither is audible. The result is a dense vocal that stays in front without the pumping of a single compressor pushed to -10 dB.

Vocal Stabilizer: an EQ that follows the voice

Vocal Stabilizer is an adaptive EQ. You choose the vocal style and register, then work on three bands with adjustable crossovers and an Amount control for each. There are two modes: Shape, which sculpts the tone, and Cut, which is more corrective.

The problem it solves is real: a voice changes colour all the time. A whispered verse is dark, a full-voice chorus opens up in the upper mids and turns harsh. A static EQ is right for one section and wrong for the other. Here the correction follows the performance instead of chasing it with automation.

The other changes

  • Vocal Assistant with Custom Flow. The assistant that builds the chain has been retuned and now takes direction: style, register, key, intensity and atmosphere, which modules to use, and an a cappella reference to match.
  • Overview page. The main controls of the most used modules on one screen.
  • Micro Pitch Shift. A new mode in the Dimension module that widens the vocal with a light detune.
  • Reverb, delay and Dimension. A Wet Level mode, three-band filters with adjustable slopes, and automatic ducking on reverb and delay, so the effect dips while the vocal sings and comes back in the gaps.
  • Redesigned interface.

The ARA formats are only for Moments. Two warnings for Logic and Ableton users. In Logic Pro, Moments only runs on Intel Macs or with Logic opened in Rosetta, so on an Apple silicon Mac you give up native performance for the whole session. In Ableton Live, Moments is not available, because Live does not support ARA. The rest of the suite works normally in both.

My verdict

Nectar 5 is the most practical update to the suite in several years. Serial Compressor and Vocal Stabilizer are not new ideas: they are two techniques engineers have always used, packed into tools that are quick to set up. That is exactly why they work. Moments, on the other hand, is genuinely new, and on a pop or R&B production it saves hours of automation.

Two cautions, as an engineer.

The assistant is a starting point. Vocal Assistant listens to the file, not the song. It does not know that the vocal should sit behind the synth in the verse and in front in the chorus, or how much room the arrangement leaves. Custom Flow makes it easier to steer, but the choices that make a vocal work in a mix still belong to whoever is listening.

The chain should not become a preset. Three stages of compression plus an adaptive EQ plus a clipper is a lot of processing. Pushed all the way, they flatten the vocal: stable, but without roundness or breath. Compression should add presence, not switch off the performance. Check in mono too, because doubles, Micro Pitch Shift and harmonies work on width and can cause phase problems.

For home producers, Nectar 5 Elements at $39 is a sensible buy. For working mix engineers, the full version is worth the sale mainly for Serial Compressor and Vocal Stabilizer as individual plugins. Moments is a strong extra reason in Pro Tools, Cubase or REAPER, and in Logic, for now, half a reason.

If you send me your vocal for mixing

If you record and pre-mix your vocal with Nectar, always send me two versions: the clean track with no processing at all, and the one with your chain as a reference. From the clean one I can build the right chain for the song; from yours I understand the character you have in mind. The same goes for effects written with Moments: print them to a separate track, so they stay yours and I can balance them in the mix. To go deeper, read about vocal compression, mixing pop and R&B vocals and phase and mono compatibility.

Frequently asked questions

When did Nectar 5 come out? The release notes are dated 23 November 2026. It has been on sale, bundles included, since 24 November.

How much does Nectar 5 cost? $249 list price, $179 on sale until 7 December 2026. Nectar 5 Elements is $55, on sale for $39. Owners of iZotope products get a dedicated upgrade price.

Does Moments work in Ableton Live? No. Moments needs ARA, which Live does not support. The other Nectar 5 modules work normally.

Does Moments work in Logic Pro on Apple silicon? Only with Logic opened in Rosetta. Natively on Apple silicon, Moments is not supported.

Does Nectar 5 replace a mix engineer? No. It speeds up the vocal work, but it does not decide how the vocal should sit in the song against everything else. That is a listening decision.
