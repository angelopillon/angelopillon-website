Why I do this work.

A song can be well written, well played and still not land. My job is to make it land.

I learned that as a musician, long before I was an engineer. At the piano, on stage with the guitar, in the studio as a session player: I watched great songs lose their power on the way from the live room to the record. What decides whether a track lands is balance, depth, dynamics and care for detail, the choices made in the control room.

Mixing and mastering are where a track becomes a record. I take a song an artist has spent months on and hand it back clearer, deeper and more powerful, without taking away what makes it theirs. After more than 1,200 delivered tracks, it is still the part of the job I love most.

My sound.

Modern, but full of life. Loud and energetic, never dull, never mechanical.

I aim for a contemporary sound that competes on streaming platforms and in clubs, and still breathes. A track can be dense and powerful without going flat: compression should add drive, not take away air, and loudness should come from the energy of the mix, not from a limiter crushing everything.

For me, roundness and melody in the sound are everything. A full, controlled low end, warm mids, silky highs, and vocals and melodic parts that always read clearly, with their shape and warmth intact. The ears of a pianist and a guitarist guide every technical choice: a record has to sing before it gets loud.

The path.

  1. 01

    The Benedetto Marcello Conservatory

    I studied piano at the Benedetto Marcello Conservatory in Venice. Classical training is the foundation of everything: harmony, dynamics, intonation, the weight of every note inside an arrangement. Twenty years of piano are still my first working tool, my ears.

  2. 02

    Guitar and live concerts

    Then modern guitar, with a focus on rock and blues, and live concerts with the band. The stage teaches what no control room can: groove, impact, the real dynamics of a cranked amp, the difference between a correct track and a living one.

  3. 03

    From hip hop to techno

    I started out producing hip hop and rap, where I learned how central the vocal and the bass are and how much the groove matters. Then techno: sound design, power in the sub, tracks that have to hold a club system for seven minutes without losing tension.

  4. 04

    Sound engineering

    Alongside that I studied sound engineering at top schools and courses in Italy and abroad: acoustics, electronics, psychoacoustics, signal flow, measurement. A rigorous method that keeps the same standard on every single track.

  5. 05

    Recording studios

    I have worked in some of the largest recording studios, on both sides of the glass. In the control room I learned from top engineers; in the live room, as an artist and a session player, I recorded guitars and productions. I know what the engineer needs and what the artist needs.

  6. 06

    Milan and Friuli

    Today I work in my own studios in Milan and in Friuli, with a hybrid analog and digital chain and Apple Digital Masters certification. From here I have delivered more than 1,200 tracks to artists, producers and labels in 66 countries.

How I work.

  • A musician’s ear

    I listen to every track as a musician first: what it wants to say, where it needs to land, what must not be touched. Technique comes after, in service of that answer.

  • An engineer’s method

    Phase and mono compatibility, stereo image, depth and three-dimensionality, loudness and true peak: every choice is measured and checked on several playback systems.

  • A studio standard

    A hybrid chain, with analog where it brings warmth, density and glue. The result has to hold up everywhere: headphones, car, club and radio.

Frequently asked questions.

Who is Angelo Pillon?

Angelo Pillon is a sound engineer specialising in mixing and mastering, with studios in Milan and Friuli, Italy. He studied piano at the Benedetto Marcello Conservatory in Venice, plays rock and blues guitar and produces hip hop and techno. For more than 15 years he has worked full time for artists, producers and labels in 66 countries, with more than 1,200 tracks delivered and an average of 4.9 out of 5 from over 700 reviews.

Where did Angelo Pillon study?

He studied piano at the Benedetto Marcello Conservatory in Venice and sound engineering at high-level schools and courses in Italy and abroad. He completed his training in recording studios, learning in the control room from top engineers and recording in the live room as an artist and session player.

What sound does he aim for in his mixes and masters?

A modern sound that is full of life: loud and energetic, never dull and never mechanical, built around roundness and melody. Phase, stereo image and three-dimensionality are handled so the track translates on every system, from headphones to club.

Which genres does he work with?

Electronic music (techno, house, deep, melodic and afro house, EDM), pop, R&B, hip hop, rap and rock. His background as a musician and producer lets him work inside the language of each genre.

Where is the studio?

Angelo Pillon works in his studios in Milan and Friuli, with Apple Digital Masters certification. Mixing and mastering happen online: tracks are sent in high quality and the final files come back ready for Spotify, Apple Music, Beatport and vinyl.
