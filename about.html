Why I do this work.

Between a good performance and a record that reaches the listener there is one decisive step. I chose to become an engineer to work exactly there.

I spent years playing: piano, guitar, rehearsal rooms with my band. That is how I learned that a song can be well written, well played and still not land. What decides whether it lands is balance, depth and dynamics: the choices made in the control room.

Mixing and mastering are where a track becomes a record. It is still the part of the job I love most: taking a song an artist has spent months on and handing it back clearer, deeper and stronger, without taking away what makes it theirs.

The path.

  1. 01

    The conservatory

    I studied classical piano at the conservatory. That is where I learned to listen: harmony, dynamics, the weight of every note inside an arrangement. Twenty years of piano are still my first working tool, my ears.

  2. 02

    Guitar and the band

    Twelve years of guitar and the years in a band taught me the energy of music played together: groove, impact, the difference between a correct track and a living one.

  3. 03

    Productions

    Producing taught me what a track is like from the inside, from the first idea to the final file. I know what it means for a songwriter to hand a song over to an engineer.

  4. 04

    Sound engineering

    Sound engineering studies gave my ears a method: acoustics, electronics, signal flow, measurement. It is what makes the same standard possible every time, not only on good days.

  5. 05

    Recording studios

    I have worked in some of the largest recording studios. There I learned the rule I still follow: the mix serves the song, not the signature of whoever mixes it.

  6. 06

    The studio in Milan

    Today I work in my own hybrid studio in Milan, Apple Digital Masters certified. From here I have delivered more than 1,200 tracks to artists, producers and labels in 66 countries.

How I work.

  • A musician’s ear

    I listen to every track as a musician first: what it wants to say, where it needs to land, what must not be touched.

  • An engineer’s method

    Phase and mono compatibility, stereo image and depth, loudness and true peak: every choice is measured and checked on several playback systems.

  • A studio standard

    A hybrid chain, with analog only where it earns its place. The result has to hold up everywhere: headphones, car, club and radio.
