Ableton Live 12.4: Link Audio, more flexible stem separation and a rebuilt Erosion

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

Ableton’s spring update takes Link from tempo to audio. If you produce electronic music and send tracks out to be mixed, there are useful stem separation changes too.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  1. What’s new
  2. What it means if you send tracks out to be mixed
  3. Sources
In short
  • Live 12.4 was released on 5 May 2026, free for Live 12 owners.
  • Link Audio streams multichannel audio in real time between compatible devices on the local network, wired or over Wi-Fi.
  • In Live Suite, stem separation works on a time selection and can combine stems into a single clip.

Ableton Link has synced tempo and phase between apps and devices for years. With version 12.4 it carries audio too.

What’s new

  • Link Audio: audio from Move, Note, Push or another computer running Live shows up as an input, with no extra hardware and no manual latency compensation.
  • Stem separation (Suite): now works on a selection in Arrangement View, so isolating 16 bars no longer means processing the whole track. Chosen stems can be combined into a single clip.
  • Updated devices: a rebuilt Erosion, a new LFO with time modes and waveforms in Delay, and updates to Chorus-Ensemble and Wavetable.
  • Learn View with lessons inside Live, and Quick Tags in the browser.
  • Move 2.0 and Note 2.0 shipped alongside 12.4, with audio tracks and Link Audio.

What it means if you send tracks out to be mixed

Separating a selection is handy for rescuing a sample from a printed loop. But when you prepare tracks for an engineer, the rule does not change: export from the original tracks, with creative effects printed and nothing on the master bus. I wrote a stem preparation guide, and for techno low end there is the one on low end in melodic techno.

Stems separated from a mix are a compromise. Stems exported from the session are a mix waiting to happen.
