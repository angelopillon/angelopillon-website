Headphone correction: IK ARC ON•EAR vs Sonarworks SoundID Reference

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

One is a box you keep in your pocket, the other software running on your computer. They do the same job in different ways.

By Angelo Pillon 29 September 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  1. The comparison
  2. Which one I would pick
  3. Sources
In short
  • ARC ON•EAR is a DAC and headphone amp with built-in correction: 250+ profiles, list price $249.99.
  • SoundID Reference for Headphones is software: a system-wide app and a plug-in, 500+ headphone models, €99.
  • For speaker simulation, ARC has it in the box; Sonarworks sells it as an add-on, Virtual Monitoring.

Headphones are not flat. Each pair has its own peaks and dips, and if you mix without knowing them you correct the song instead of the headphones. That is what correction is for.

The comparison

ARC ON•EAR SoundID Reference for Headphones
Type hardware: DAC and amp software: app and plug-in
Headphone profiles 250+ 500+
Speaker simulation included (Studio Simulation) Virtual Monitoring, sold separately
Works without a computer yes, even from a phone no
Price $249.99 €99

ARC ON•EAR. ESS SABRE 32-bit converters, a 200 mW amp, USB-C and analogue inputs, five stored presets. The profiles are measured through the same amp circuit as the box, which is its strong point. Version 1.3 in March 2026 expanded the database.

SoundID Reference. It runs as a system-wide app, so it corrects Spotify and YouTube too, and as a plug-in in your DAW. It offers zero-latency and linear-phase modes and a custom target curve.

Which one I would pick

If you mix on a desktop and want to spend less, SoundID Reference. If you work on the move, from a laptop, a tablet or on a train, ARC ON•EAR: the correction travels with the headphones, and you never forget to bypass a plug-in before you export.

Correction does not turn headphones into monitors. It tells you the truth about what you are hearing.

Either way, check on at least one other system before you deliver. I wrote a guide to mixing on headphones without surprises.
