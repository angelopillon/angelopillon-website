<ul>
<li>Mid is what the two channels share; side is the difference between them.</li>
<li>The safest moves: cut lows on the sides, add air on the sides, presence in the centre.</li>
<li>Small moves, 0.5 to 2 dB, with a mono check after each one.</li>
</ul></div>

In a typical mix, kick, bass, vocal and snare sit in the centre, while pads, reverbs, wide guitars and backing vocals sit on the sides. Mid/side EQ lets you fix one part without touching the other: lift the vocal without lifting the cymbals, open a pad without weakening the bass.

## Mono lows: the most useful cut

Frequencies below 100 to 150 Hz on the sides almost never carry anything good: reverb, incoherent phase, noise. A gentle high-pass on the side channel between 80 and 150 Hz makes the low end tighter and louder, and the master holds up better in clubs and on vinyl. It is the first check I make on electronic tracks; more in [phase and mono compatibility](/phase-and-mono-compatibility).

## Air on the sides

A gentle 1 to 2 dB shelf above 10 kHz on the side channel opens the stereo image without making the vocal, which sits in the centre, any harsher. It sounds more natural than a stereo widener because it does not shift phase.

## Presence in the centre

If the vocal is slightly buried, 0.5 to 1.5 dB between 2 and 4 kHz on the mid brings it forward without touching the cymbals and synths on the sides. Likewise, a small cut between 200 and 400 Hz on the mid cleans the centre when kick, bass and vocal pile up.

<blockquote class="pull">Mid/side is a scalpel. If you need more than 2 dB, the problem is in the mix, and that is where to fix it.</blockquote>

## The risks

- **Too wide:** boosting the sides a lot sounds huge on headphones, but in mono the side content drops and the balance shifts.
- **Moving the vocal:** heavy mid EQ changes the tone of everything in the centre, not just the vocal.
- **Unbalanced lows:** a side high-pass set too high can strip the body from pads and wide guitars.

## How to check

After every move, listen in mono and compare at matched loudness. The track has to stay balanced on a bluetooth speaker and on a phone. The site's [stereo and phase analyzer](/tools/stereo-phase-analyzer) shows correlation and the balance between centre and sides.
