<li>Mono lows below 120 to 150 Hz, with kick and bass not masking each other.</li>

I work on a lot of afro house, and it is a genre that is easy to ruin in mastering. Congas, shakers, djembes and toms are full of transients: if the limiter flattens them, the groove loses its push and the track gets louder but less alive.

## Percussion first

The first thing I listen to is how the percussion breathes in the drop. If the mix is already dense, I prefer to shave the peaks with a light clipper or with tape before the limiter, so the final limiter works 1 to 2 dB instead of 4 to 5. The result is louder and keeps the attack.

## A tight low end

In afro house the kick is soft and long, and the bass often plays with it. A side high-pass below 120 to 150 Hz keeps the lows mono, and checking how kick and bass sum prevents the drop from bloating on a club system. If kick and bass are not tuned to the key, mastering makes it obvious: I cover that in [tuning kick and 808](/tuning-kick-and-808).

<blockquote class="pull">A good afro house master does not sound loud. It sounds like it pushes, and the floor follows.</blockquote>

## Vocals and ethnic instruments

Many tracks have chants, vocals in African languages, kalimba, flutes, marimba. These are rich sounds between 1 and 5 kHz that can turn harsh after the limiter. A light dynamic EQ in that range keeps them smooth in the loudest passages without dulling them elsewhere.

## Loudness: club and streaming

| Destination | Reference |
|---|---|
| Club and Beatport | −8 / −6 LUFS short-term in the drops |
| Streaming | the same master: the platforms turn it down themselves |
| True peak | −0.3 / −0.2 dBTP |

I usually deliver one master that works in both contexts. In [Beatport vs Spotify mastering](/beatport-vs-spotify-mastering) I explain when it makes sense to make two.

## Before sending your track to mastering

- Leave at least 3 to 6 dB of headroom on the mix, with no limiter on the bus.
- Check that the drop is not already compressed to the limit.
- Send an afro house reference you love: it helps more than any description.
