<li>In 2025 US vinyl sales passed one billion dollars for the first time since 1983: 46.8 million records.</li>
<li>Vinyl is not “more faithful” than a lossless file. It is different, with its own sound, limits and ritual.</li>

According to the RIAA’s annual report, US vinyl sales grew for the nineteenth year in a row, up 9.3% in 2025, and now make up nearly half of the format’s global value. Vinyl brought in more than three times the revenue of CDs.

## What vinyl does better

**The master.** Many records get a dedicated master, less squashed than the streaming one. That is why vinyl can sound more dynamic and open. The credit goes to the decision, not the medium.

**The listening.** You drop the needle, play a whole side, look at the sleeve. You listen more closely, and attention changes everything.

## What vinyl does worse

**Low end.** Very low frequencies have to sit in the centre and cannot be too loud, or the needle jumps.

**Highs and sibilance.** Loud “s” sounds distort, and detail drops towards the centre of the record.

**Noise.** Crackle, dust and scratches are part of the charm, not of the fidelity.

| | Vinyl | Lossless streaming |
|---|---|---|
| Fidelity to the original | high, with physical limits | identical to the master file |
| Dynamics | often wider thanks to a dedicated master | depends on the master |
| Background noise | present | none |
| Experience | ritual, object, artwork | convenience, endless catalogue |

<blockquote class="pull">Vinyl does not sound better because it is vinyl. It sounds better when someone decided to make it sound better.</blockquote>
