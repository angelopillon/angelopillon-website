Almost every artist I work with asks me this: “how much do I earn if my song gets a hundred thousand streams?”. It is a fair question, but the answer is not a fixed number. Spotify has no per-stream rate: it splits a pie. What reaches you depends on how big the pie is, how much of it you get and who stands between you and Spotify.

Here is how it works, without myths or promises.

## How the money is split

Premium subscriptions and advertising from free users go into a single pool, calculated separately for each country. Spotify keeps roughly 30% and the rest goes to rights holders: labels, distributors and publishers.

The split is based on how much each track was played. If a song gets 1% of a country’s streams in a month, it receives 1% of that country’s pool. This is the so-called pro rata model.

That explains why the value of a stream varies so much:

- **Country.** A subscription costs more in Switzerland than in India, so the Swiss pool is richer.
- **Account type.** Premium subscribers generate more revenue than free users.
- **Month.** If there are more total streams in a month, each stream is worth slightly less.

## Who gets what

The money does not go straight to the artist. There are two separate flows:

**Recording royalties** (the master). They go to the label or distributor, which passes them on to the artist according to the contract. An independent artist using a digital distributor receives most of this share; an artist signed to a label receives the percentage in their contract.

**Publishing royalties** (the composition). They go to songwriters and publishers, through collecting societies. If you wrote the song, it is important to register it, or this part is lost.

## The 1,000-stream threshold

Since 2024 a track must exceed 1,000 streams in the last 12 months, with a minimum number of unique listeners, to share in recording royalties. Spotify does not say what that minimum is.

The stated aim is to move money from tracks that are almost never played to those with a real audience, and to discourage people uploading thousands of tracks to collect fractions of a cent. The threshold does not affect publishing royalties for songwriters and publishers, which are still paid.

At the same time Spotify introduced penalties for distributors when a track receives artificial streams, and stricter rules for very short noise and ambient-sound tracks.

## So, how much?

| Streams | Estimate at $0.004 |
|---|---|
| 1,000 | about $4 |
| 10,000 | about $40 |
| 100,000 | about $400 |
| 1,000,000 | about $4,000 |

It is a rough estimate, before what the label or distributor keeps. The real figure is only in your distributor’s statements, broken down by country and platform.

To give a sense of scale: according to figures released by Spotify, in 2025 more than 13,800 artists earned at least $100,000 from the platform. That is a lot of people, but a small share of everyone who releases music.

## The most common myths

**“Spotify pays X cents a stream.”** There is no fixed rate. There is an average that changes every month.

**“A million streams makes you rich.”** A million streams, depending on where they come from, is worth a few thousand dollars before deductions. A great result, but not a salary.

**“Buying streams is worth it.”** No. Artificial streams are detected and removed, and can lead to the track being taken down and penalties for the distributor.

## How to really earn more

**Get replays.** Streaming rewards being played many times. A song that gets saved to playlists and keeps coming back is worth far more than one heard once.

**Register the song as a songwriter.** It is a separate royalty stream, and many independent artists forget it.

**Plan the release.** Pitching the track to Spotify’s editors at least 7 days ahead gets it into your followers’ Release Radar. More in the guide to [releasing your first song on Spotify](/how-to-release-your-first-song-on-spotify).

**Diversify.** Shows, merch, vinyl, licensing for video and advertising. For most artists streaming is a shop window more than the main source of income.

<blockquote class="pull">Streaming pays those who get played many times. And a song gets replayed when it sounds good.</blockquote>

## Sound matters

Streams do not depend on sound alone, but a track that holds up next to the ones around it in a playlist starts with an advantage. If your song sounds smaller, duller or muddier than the previous one, listeners skip it. And a skip in the first seconds is a lost stream. To understand how platforms adjust volume, read [why volume changes between songs](/why-volume-changes-between-songs-on-spotify).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Apple Music pay more?** On average it usually does, because it has no free ad-supported tier. But there too the value changes with country and month.

**Are tracks under 1,000 streams worth nothing?** For recording royalties on Spotify, yes. Publishing royalties still accrue.

**When do payments arrive?** It depends on the distributor. Usually a few months after the streams.
