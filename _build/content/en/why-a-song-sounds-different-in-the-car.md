“It sounded great in the studio, not in the car.” It is the line I hear most. It is also why I check every track on several systems before delivering it.

## What happens on each system

**Phone.** The speakers are tiny: below a certain frequency they reproduce almost nothing. You only hear the kick and bass through their harmonics, the higher frequencies they generate.

**Car.** The cabin is small and closed, and some low frequencies boom. Engine and tyre noise cover details and low sounds. The speakers sit low and to the sides, so the stereo image shifts.

**Headphones.** They block outside noise and every detail feels closer. But each ear only hears its own channel, so stereo sounds wider than it is.

**Living room speakers.** The room adds reflections and boosts some notes more than others.

| System | Typical problem | What the mix must ensure |
|---|---|---|
| Phone | bass disappears | harmonics on kick and bass |
| Car | boomy lows, noise | controlled low end, clear vocal |
| Headphones | exaggerated stereo | good mono compatibility |
| Living room | room boom | tonal balance |

<blockquote class="pull">A good mix does not sound perfect anywhere. It sounds good everywhere.</blockquote>
