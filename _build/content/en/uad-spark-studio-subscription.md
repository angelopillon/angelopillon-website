For years Universal Audio plug-ins had one constraint: they only ran on UA hardware, Apollo interfaces or dedicated DSP. Then native versions arrived, running directly on the computer’s processor, and with them the Spark subscription. In September 2026 Universal Audio went a step further: Spark became Spark Studio, a bundle that also includes their DAW, LUNA.

Good news for beginners, but the question is the usual one: is it better to pay monthly or buy plug-ins once? I run the numbers and explain how to think about it.

## What is inside Spark Studio

**LUNA Studio.** Universal Audio’s DAW, designed for recording and arranging with a workflow similar to an analogue studio. Bought separately it costs $199.

**The native UAD library.** More than 60 plug-ins: console, compressor, EQ, reverb and tape emulations. Neve, Lexicon, Teletronix, SSL and Studer are all there.

**Virtual instruments.** Synths and instruments for writing directly in LUNA or in your DAW.

**New plug-ins.** Releases during your subscription are included, like the recent Capitol Studios.

No UA hardware is needed: everything runs on the computer.

## The numbers

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Spark Studio monthly | $19.99 |
| Spark Studio yearly | $149.99 |
| LUNA Studio alone | $199 one-off |
| Capitol Studios alone | $249 one-off |

A single flagship plug-in at full price costs more than a year of subscription. It looks like a bargain, and partly it is. But there is an important detail: plug-ins you buy stay yours; subscription plug-ins disappear when you stop paying, and so does the ability to reopen old sessions exactly as you left them.

An example. Over three years the yearly plan costs about $450. With the same money, waiting for sales, you can buy several UAD plug-ins you will use forever. If instead you use many different plug-ins on every project, the subscription stays cheaper.

## Subscription or purchase: how to decide

**How many sessions must you be able to reopen in years?** If you work for clients who may ask for a revision months later, a subscription ties you in: stop paying and those sessions no longer sound the same.

**How many plug-ins do you actually use?** Most producers end up using the same ones: a compressor, an EQ, a reverb, a tape. If that is you, buy them.

**Are you still looking for your sound?** Then a subscription is perfect: try everything, find out what you need, then decide.

**Do you work in LUNA?** Spark Studio becomes almost a given, because it gives you the DAW and the plug-ins together.

<blockquote class="pull">If a project has to reopen in five years, the plug-ins it uses have to still be there in five years.</blockquote>

## When it pays off

**Yes,** if you are building your chain and want to try everything before deciding, if you work in LUNA, or if you are starting out and do not want to spend hundreds in one go.

**No,** if you use three or four UAD plug-ins in every session: buy them on sale and they are yours forever.

**A middle way:** subscribe for a few months, try everything, then cancel and buy only the ones you really used.

## Plug-ins and analogue

UAD plug-ins are among the best emulations around. I use them every day alongside real outboard. But an emulation is still an emulation: an analogue machine’s behaviour, its small differences, how it reacts to hot levels, are most audible in mastering. That is why my chain has both.

## A practical tip if you send tracks to an engineer

When you send me your tracks, print the effects that are part of the sound. If the reverb on a vocal or the saturation on a synth is a creative choice, export the track with the effect. That way the mix does not depend on what is or is not on my computer. Everything is in the guide to [stem preparation](/stem-preparation-guide).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an Apollo?** No, Spark Studio works without UA hardware.

**Does it work in my DAW?** The plug-ins come in standard formats and run in the main DAWs, not just LUNA.

**What about existing Spark subscribers?** They move to Spark Studio at no extra cost.
