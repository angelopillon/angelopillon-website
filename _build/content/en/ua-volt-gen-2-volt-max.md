<div class="tldr"><span class="box__title">In short</span>
<ul>
<li>Volt Gen 2 and Volt Max have been available since 1 October 2026, from $149.</li>
<li>All of them have 32-bit/192 kHz conversion and Tone Shaping preamps with three modes: Warmth, Focus and Shine.</li>
<li>The Max models add six compression presets modelled on the 1176, LA-2A and VCA compressors.</li>
</ul></div>

LUNA 3.0 arrived alongside the interfaces, with LUNA Play, a simplified view for writing and recording. The Volts can be controlled straight from UA’s DAW.

## The models

| Model | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Volt 1 Gen 2 | the base model, no display or Auto-Gain |
| Volt 2 Max | Max processing |
| Volt 4 Gen 2 | ADAT optical input |
| Volt 4 Max | Max processing and ADAT |
| Volt 4P Max | Max processing |

UA speaks of six models; the press release names five. All except the Volt 1 have Auto-Gain, Clip Protection and a display with gain-reduction metering. MIDI, a talkback mic and speaker switching are on selected models.

**Tone Shaping.** Warmth recalls tubes, Focus solid state, Shine British Class A.

**Max.** Six one-touch dynamics presets, in real time with no added latency.

## My advice

Compressing on the way in helps you get a solid take, but you cannot undo it. If you are recording vocals for someone else to mix, keep compression light or use it on the monitor path only. Leave at least 6 dB of headroom on the peaks: a clean, dynamic file gives me far more options than a squashed one. More in the guide to [gain staging](/gain-staging-in-your-daw).

<blockquote class="pull">Compression on the way in helps the performer. In the mix, what you did not print can always be added.</blockquote>
