Universal Audio built the plug-in with the Capitol team. The Capitol Tower in Hollywood opened in 1956, and its underground echo chambers are among the most famous in recording history.

## What it does

**The rooms.** Studios A and B, with microphones you can drag around the room while the space responds in real time.

**The mics.** A “Historic Mic Locker” of vintage microphones, with setups curated by Capitol alum Steve Genewick.

**The Decca Tree.** The classic three-mic array for strings, horns and vocal groups.

**The echo chambers.** The four chambers under the tower, usable as a reverb in their own right.

**The presets.** Settings from past and present Capitol engineers, producers and musicians.

## How I would use it in a mix

A room plug-in is not a reverb to put on everything. I reach for one when a close-miked source, a vocal or a synth, has to sound as if it was played in a real place. The trick is a little signal and a lot of consistency: one room for the elements that belong together, and the echo chamber on a send for the tails.

<blockquote class="pull">One real room gives depth. Three different rooms just give confusion.</blockquote>

Before adding space, listen to whether the mix already has its own [depth](/depth-in-the-mix). Often, taking reverb away from the front elements lets the ones behind breathe, as I explain in the article on [reverb and delay](/reverb-and-delay-in-the-mix).
