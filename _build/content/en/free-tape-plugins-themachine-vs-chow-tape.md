<div class="tldr"><span class="box__title">In short</span>
<ul>
<li>Analog Obsession TheMachine is free from Patreon, in VST3, AU and AAX for Mac and Windows.</li>
<li>CHOW Tape is open source, born as a physical model of the Sony TC-260 and now more flexible.</li>
<li>For mastering, and for specific machines with calibration, paid emulations are still the better choice.</li>
</ul></div>

Tape adds harmonics, rounds off transients and gives the low end density. My mastering chain has a real tape machine in it; in mixes I often use emulations, track by track.

## The comparison

| | TheMachine | CHOW Tape |
|---|---|---|
| Price | free | free, open source |
| Speed | 7.5 and 15 ips | more options |
| Controls | Input, Drive, Output, Speed, Wow & Flutter, Noise, Bias | many physical-model parameters |
| Formats | VST3, AU, AAX Native and AudioSuite | also CLAP and LV2 |
| Reference machine | not stated | based on the Sony TC-260 |

**TheMachine.** The input is gain-compensated, so you can push the drive without level jumps, and the output is clean. Noise runs from -120 to -40 dB. Rare for a free plug-in, it also includes AudioSuite for Pro Tools.

**CHOW Tape.** More parameters, more machine and tape types. It takes longer, but goes from subtle to lo-fi.

## How I would use them

On drums and bass, a touch of tape holds transients together without a compressor. On digital synths it takes the edge off. TheMachine is perfect when you want to decide fast; CHOW Tape when you are after a specific character. I would keep them off the master: there you want named, calibrated and predictable machines.

<blockquote class="pull">You do not hear tape when you add it. You hear it when you take it away.</blockquote>
