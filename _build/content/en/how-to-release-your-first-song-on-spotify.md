Releasing a song today is easier than ever. You do not need a label, a contract or a press office: just a finished track, a distributor and a few hours of well-done work. But precisely because it is easy, tens of thousands of tracks come out every day. The details make the difference: a ready master, the right data, a date chosen in advance.

Every week I deliver masters to artists about to release. This is the checklist I give everyone, in the right order.

## 1. Finish the song

Mix and master must be final before you upload. Changing the file after release is only possible by re-releasing the track, and you lose the numbers it has built.

Before calling it finished, listen to the master on several systems: headphones, phone, car. Compare it with two or three songs in your genre that sit in the playlists you would like to land on.

Deliver the master as WAV, in the format your distributor asks for: more in the article on [master delivery formats](/master-delivery-formats).

## 2. Choose a distributor

DistroKid, CD Baby, TuneCore and many others deliver your track to every service. Prices, commissions and extras differ: some charge a yearly fee, others a fee per release, others take a percentage.

What to look at:

- **The pricing model**, based on how many tracks you plan to release.
- **What happens if you stop paying**: with some, the track is taken down.
- **Included services**: automatic revenue splits between collaborators, publishing royalty collection, synced lyrics.

The distributor also assigns the ISRC, the “number plate” that identifies the recording, and the release’s UPC.

## 3. Prepare the material

- **Square artwork**, usually at least 3000 x 3000 pixels, with no platform logos, links or disallowed text.
- **Credits**: songwriters, producers, performers, spelled correctly. They matter for royalties too.
- **Metadata**: title, an artist name identical on every release, genre, language, explicit version if any.
- **Lyrics**, if you want them to show while the song plays.

A typo in the artist name can land your track on the profile of someone else with the same name. Check twice.

## 4. Set the date

Upload the track at least three or four weeks before release. The distributor needs time to deliver it to the services, and you need time to pitch it to playlists and prepare promotion.

Many releases come out on Friday, the day platforms refresh many new-music playlists.

## 5. Pitch it to playlists

In Spotify for Artists you can pitch an unreleased track to editorial playlist curators, one at a time. Describe the song concretely: genre, mood, instruments, who it is for.

If you do it at least 7 days before release, the track enters your followers’ Release Radar. Pitching does not guarantee placement, but it is free and should never be skipped.

## 6. Prepare promotion

- **Claim your profile** on Spotify for Artists and the artist tools of other services.
- **Set up a pre-save link**, so people can save the song before release.
- **Prepare content**: short videos, photos, behind the scenes of the production.
- **Tell your followers**: mailing list, socials, friends and collaborators.

## The calendar

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 4-6 weeks before | master finished, artwork ready |
| 3-4 weeks before | upload to the distributor |
| 2-3 weeks before | profile claimed, pre-save live |
| at least 7 days before | pitch in Spotify for Artists |
| release day | share, thank, watch the data |
| weeks after | content, independent playlists, next song |

<blockquote class="pull">Release day is prepared a month in advance.</blockquote>

## The most common mistakes

**Uploading at the last minute.** No pitch, no Release Radar, no time for promotion.

**A master that is not ready.** A song quieter, duller or muddier than the others in a playlist gets skipped.

**Paying for streams or guaranteed playlists.** Artificial plays are detected and can get the track removed.

**Releasing and disappearing.** The algorithm watches how the audience reacts in the first weeks. Keep the song in the conversation.

To understand how earnings work, read [how much Spotify pays per stream](/how-much-does-spotify-pay-per-stream).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I release without a distributor?** On the big services, no: you need a distributor or a label.

**How much does it cost?** From a few tens of euros a year, depending on the distributor and number of releases.

**Can I change the file after release?** Only by re-releasing the track as a new release.
