<div class="tldr"><span class="box__title">In short</span>
<ul>
<li>Spotify brings every track to a similar average level, -14 LUFS on the normal setting.</li>
<li>Premium subscribers can choose Loud (-11 LUFS), Normal (-14) or Quiet (-19), or switch normalisation off.</li>
<li>Apple Music uses a lower level, -16 LUFS. A track can also seem louder or quieter because of how it was mastered.</li>
</ul></div>

LUFS measure perceived loudness, not peaks. Platforms use them to turn down tracks that are too loud and, in some cases, turn up quiet ones. That way you move from one song to the next without touching the volume.

## How it works on Spotify

Spotify measures a track when it is uploaded but does not change it: it adjusts the volume while you listen. For albums it uses a single value for every track, so quieter songs stay as quiet as the artist intended. The web player and many third-party devices, such as TVs and speakers, do not apply normalisation.

| Setting | Level |
|---|---|
| Loud | -11 LUFS |
| Normal | -14 LUFS |
| Quiet | -19 LUFS |

## So why do I still hear differences?

**The master.** Two tracks at the same average level can feel different: a heavily compressed one sounds “fuller”, a dynamic one livelier and more open.

**The genre.** A ballad and a techno track have different frequency content, and the ear perceives them differently even at the same level.

**The platform.** Apple Music normalises to -16 LUFS, so the same track may sound slightly quieter than on Spotify.
