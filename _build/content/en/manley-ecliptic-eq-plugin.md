<li>Manley ecliptic is an original EQ, not a model of an existing unit: mid-side tone shaping with a single set of controls.</li>
<li>It is built on analogue transformer, filter and EQ models, developed with Manley’s hardware designers and the Brainworx and Plugin Alliance teams.</li>

In July 2026 Brainworx founder Dirk Ulrich signed the agreements to take Brainworx and Plugin Alliance back after the Native Instruments insolvency. Both brands joined RCKFRC, the family-owned holding that also controls Manley Labs and Apogee. I covered the background in the article on [iZotope, Boris FX and Native Instruments](/izotope-boris-fx-native-instruments).

## What we know

**A mid-side EQ with one set of controls.** Not two separate EQs for middle and sides, but a single approach that shapes both.

**Analogue models.** Modelled transformers, filters and equalisers, using Brainworx’s TMT technology.

**Plug-in first, hardware later.** Ulrich explained that the plug-in is a way to gather feedback: if demand is strong enough, a hardware version goes on Manley’s roadmap.

The 6 November announcement letter gives no price, formats or release date.

## Why it interests me

Mid-side is a powerful mastering tool, and an easy one to overdo: you widen the sides, lift the top, and the centre loses body. An EQ that thinks about middle and sides with one set of controls could make choices more musical and less risky. The usual rule still applies: after every move on the sides, check [mono compatibility](/phase-and-mono-compatibility).

<blockquote class="pull">Mid-side is not for making everything wider. It is for putting everything in its place.</blockquote>
