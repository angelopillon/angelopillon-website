## What lossless means

A “lossy” file such as MP3 or AAC removes the parts of the sound that, according to a model of hearing, you would notice least. A lossless file, such as FLAC or ALAC, is compressed like a zip archive: smaller than the original, but bit-for-bit identical when you play it.

## The services compared

| | Maximum quality | Extra cost |
|---|---|---|
| Spotify Premium | 24-bit/44.1 kHz, FLAC | none |
| Apple Music | 24-bit/192 kHz (Hi-Res Lossless), ALAC | none |
| Tidal | 24-bit/192 kHz, FLAC | included in the plan |

Spotify began rolling out lossless on 10 September 2025, reaching more than 50 markets by October. You switch it on manually on each device, in the audio quality settings. On Apple Music, Lossless runs from 16-bit/44.1 kHz to 24-bit/48 kHz; above 48 kHz it is called Hi-Res Lossless and needs an external converter.

## Can you hear it?

The biggest jump is not between lossless and hi-res, but between distracted and attentive listening. CD quality, 16-bit/44.1 kHz, is already beyond what most people can perceive. What matters more:

- **The connection.** Bluetooth does not have enough bandwidth for FLAC: with most wireless headphones, lossless does not arrive intact.
- **Your headphones or speakers.** A good pair of wired headphones makes more difference than any format.
- **The master.** A well mixed and mastered song sounds good even in AAC. A badly made one stays badly made in hi-res.

<blockquote class="pull">The format matters. What is inside the file matters more.</blockquote>
