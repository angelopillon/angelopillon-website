<ul>
<li>The codec is how your phone compresses music to send it to wireless headphones.</li>
<li>iPhones use AAC; many Android phones also support aptX and LDAC, which send more data.</li>
<li>Only aptX Lossless reaches CD quality with no loss, and both phone and headphones must support it.</li>

People often ask me whether lossless on Spotify or Apple Music is worth switching on with wireless headphones. The answer depends on a piece of tech almost nobody looks at: the codec.

## The main codecs

| Codec | Where you find it | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| SBC | every device | basic |
| AAC | iPhone, iPad | good, around 256 kbps |
| aptX | many Android phones | up to 352 kbps, lossy |
| LDAC | many Android phones, Sony headphones | up to 990 kbps, lossy |
| aptX Lossless | some recent Android phones and headphones | lossless CD quality |

## What it means for you

**If you have an iPhone.** The connection runs on AAC even if your headphones support aptX or LDAC. Well-implemented AAC sounds very good: no drama.

**If you have an Android.** Check in the developer options which codec is active. LDAC at its highest quality needs a stable signal.

**Lossless streaming.** Standard Bluetooth cannot carry it intact, because there is not enough bandwidth. To really hear it you need wired headphones or speakers connected over Wi-Fi.

<blockquote class="pull">The best headphones on the worst codec still sound better than the worst headphones on the best codec.</blockquote>

## An engineer’s advice

The headphones matter before the codec: their frequency response, how they fit, how much they isolate. Many consumer headphones boost bass and treble to sound more exciting. That is fine for listening, not for judging a mix. If you make music, read the guide to [mixing on headphones](/mixing-on-headphones).
