Master delivery formats: bit depth, sample rate, dither and metadata

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

A perfect master can reach the platforms damaged by the wrong format or missing metadata. Here is what I prepare for every delivery and what to check before you upload.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  2. Dither

In short
  • For distribution: 24-bit WAV at the project sample rate, or whatever your distributor asks for.
  • Dither only when you reduce bit depth, for example from 24 to 16, and only once, at the very end.
  • Clear names, ISRC codes and correct metadata prevent errors and release delays.

Mastering ends with a file, and that file has to reach Spotify, Apple Music, Beatport and every other service intact. A few checks avoid the most common surprises.

Bit depth and sample rate

Almost every distributor now accepts 24-bit WAV, and many accept sample rates above 44.1 kHz. My advice: work and deliver at the project sample rate, with no pointless conversions. If the distributor asks for 16-bit / 44.1 kHz, convert once, at the end, with a good converter and dither.

The Apple Digital Masters badge requires 24-bit files and an Apple-approved mastering engineer.

Dither

When you reduce resolution, for example from 24 to 16 bits, the last bits are truncated and create very low-level distortion. Dither adds an inaudible noise that turns it into a neutral hiss. Simple rules:

  • Use it only when reducing bit depth.
  • Once, as the last step in the chain.
  • Never on files that will be processed again, such as stems for mixing.
The format will not improve a master. The wrong one can make it worse, and you will not notice until the track is live.

Every master has its ceiling at −0.3/−0.2 dBTP, checked after a test encode too.

File names and metadata

  • A clear name used everywhere: Artist - Title (Extended Mix).wav.
  • A different ISRC code for each version: original, extended, instrumental.
  • Title, artists, featuring and remixers written exactly as on the artwork.
  • Check that the file starts and ends clean: no clicks, no extra silence.

Before you upload

Listen to the master from start to finish, check loudness and true peak with the LUFS meter, and compare the uploaded file with the distributor’s preview. If anything sounds different, stop before confirming the release.
