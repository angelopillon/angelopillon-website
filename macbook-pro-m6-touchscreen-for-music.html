M6 MacBook Pro and the touchscreen MacBook Pro: what matters for music

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

Two new laptops in one week: one faster at the usual price, one with a touchscreen. Before you move your studio onto a new Mac, there are three things to check.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  1. M6 MacBook Pro
  2. Touchscreen MacBook Pro
  3. Three checks before you buy
  4. What actually matters for the mix

In short
  • 14-inch MacBook Pro with M6: 12-core CPU, 16 to 32 GB of memory, three Thunderbolt 4 ports, from $1,999.
  • Touchscreen MacBook Pro: OLED display, M5 Pro or M5 Max, from $2,899 for the 14-inch.
  • Both ship with macOS 27 Golden Gate: check your DAW, plug-ins and drivers before using them for paid work.

At the end of October Apple announced two MacBook Pro models a day apart. Both have been shipping since 6 November 2026.

M6 MacBook Pro

  • 12-core CPU, two more cores than M5.
  • 16 to 32 GB of unified memory, up to 170 GB/s of bandwidth.
  • 1 TB base storage, three Thunderbolt 4 ports, HDMI, SD slot, headphone jack.
  • From $1,999: $400 more than the base M5, with twice the storage.

Touchscreen MacBook Pro

It is the first Mac with a touchscreen. It uses an OLED panel and the same M5 Pro and M5 Max chips as the March models, which stay on sale. Prices start at $2,899 (14-inch, M5 Pro) and $3,399 for the 16-inch. In Logic Pro the screen works as a keyboard, pads and a control surface.

Three checks before you buy

  1. macOS 27 compatibility. New Macs ship with Golden Gate and normally cannot be downgraded. Pro Tools, for example, does not officially support it yet.
  2. Older plug-ins. Some still need Rosetta 2, which the system does not install on its own.
  3. A real session on day one. Open a full project with every plug-in you use, and keep your old Mac ready until everything is confirmed.

What actually matters for the mix

For mixing and mastering, the processor matters less than people think. Monitoring, the room and the audio interface matter more. Before spending $2,000 on a laptop, read my home studio budget guide: the upgrade that changes your sound the most is often somewhere else.

A faster Mac does not make the mix sound better. It makes the wait shorter.
