Setting the mastering limiter, step by step

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

The limiter is the last link in the chain and the one that decides whether a master is loud and clean or loud and tiring. The settings matter less than how much work you ask of it.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read

Time20 minutesLevelIntermediateYou needA true peak limiter and a loudness meter

A limiter does one thing: it stops the signal from crossing a ceiling. The catch is that it does so by pulling peaks down, and when the peaks are many and loud it starts squashing the music. A good loud master is built before the limiter: a balanced mix, with compression and saturation that have already shaved the sharpest peaks.

The procedure

  1. Set the ceiling to −0.3/−0.2 dBTP

    With a true peak limiter using oversampling, a ceiling of −0.3 or −0.2 dBTP keeps the file safe from distortion after encoding without giving away loudness. I explain why in the article on true peak.

  2. Turn on oversampling and true peak

    Without oversampling the limiter only watches the samples, and the peaks between samples slip through. 4x is a good starting point.

  3. Attack and lookahead

    A lookahead between 1 and 5 ms lets the limiter prepare for the peak. Shorter keeps transients, longer is cleaner on the low end.

  4. Release

    Start with auto release. Too short distorts the bass, too long makes the track pump after every kick.

  5. Raise the gain in small steps

    Watch the gain reduction on the loudest passages. Up to 2 or 3 dB is almost always transparent; past 4 or 5 dB you hear the kick lose its punch and the mix close in. If you need more, work before the limiter.

  6. Measure the loudness

    Check integrated and short-term LUFS and true peak across the whole track. The site's LUFS meter does it in your browser.

  7. Compare at matched loudness

    Turn the master's output down to match the loudness of the original mix and compare. If the master sounds smaller than the mix at the same level, the limiter is working too hard.

Starting values

Parameter Value
Ceiling −0.3 / −0.2 dBTP
Oversampling 4x or more
Lookahead 1 to 5 ms
Release auto, then by ear
Gain reduction on peaks 2 to 3 dB, rarely over 4
The limiter does not create loudness. It protects it. Loudness is built earlier, in the mix and the mastering chain.

Two limiters in series

On very loud tracks I sometimes use two stages: a clipper or gentle limiter that takes 1 to 2 dB off the fastest peaks, then the final limiter. Each works lightly, and the result is cleaner than one limiter doing 5 dB.
