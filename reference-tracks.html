Reference tracks: how to actually use them in mixing and mastering

Read inItalianoEnglishDeutsch

A good reference is the fastest way to know whether your mix works. Compared at the wrong level, it will lead you astray in seconds.

By Angelo Pillon 9 November 2026 3 min read
On this page

On this page

  1. Choosing references
  2. Match the loudness
  3. What to listen for
  4. Common mistakes
  5. Send them to your engineer too
In short
  • Pick 2 or 3 tracks in the same genre, with an arrangement close to yours.
  • Match loudness before comparing: the louder track always sounds better.
  • Compare one thing at a time: low end, vocal, stereo, highs, dynamics.

After two hours on the same song, your ears get used to anything. A reference resets your listening: within seconds it tells you whether the low end is too heavy, the vocal too far back or the highs too harsh. As long as you use it the right way.

Choosing references

  • Same genre and energy: a ballad is not a reference for a melodic techno drop.
  • Similar arrangement: a track with a full vocal does not compare to an instrumental.
  • A master you know well: you have heard it on many systems and know how it should sound.
  • Good-quality files: WAV or high-quality streaming, not an MP3 ripped from YouTube.

Match the loudness

References are finished masters, almost always louder than your mix. Compared like that, your mix sounds small and you are tempted to push everything up. Turn the reference down until its perceived loudness matches your mix, ideally measured in LUFS. The site’s A/B reference player matches loudness automatically.

Comparing at different levels is not comparing. It is rewarding whatever is louder.

What to listen for

Area Question to ask
Low end Do kick and bass carry the same weight? Is the bass longer or shorter?
Vocal Does it sit at the same level above the beat? Brighter or darker?
Stereo Is my track narrower or wider? What lives on the sides?
Highs Are the cymbals harsher? Is there more air?
Dynamics Does the chorus or drop lift by the same amount?

Change one thing at a time and go back to the reference after each change, for a few seconds. Long listens re-train your ears to the reference.

Common mistakes

  • Copying the reference: it calibrates your ears, it is not a sound to clone. Your track has a different arrangement.
  • Too many references: with ten different songs every decision feels wrong. Two or three are enough.
  • Comparing a mix with a master: in the mix, judge balance and space, not level. Loudness comes with mastering.

Send them to your engineer too

When you send a track for mixing or mastering, include one or two references and write what you like in each: “the kick in this one”, “the vocal in that one”. It is the most useful information you can give.
